About

Security that holds up in the real world

I started in DevSecOps, building security checks into CI/CD pipelines and running penetration tests, which taught me how vulnerabilities actually get into software. Today at Johnson & Johnson I'm on the other side of that: investigating alerts, tuning detections so analysts aren't buried in noise, and working with system owners until fixes are verified.

The thread through all of it is automation. If I do something twice, I script it. That habit cut up to 40% of the manual work on my teams, and it's why I spend my own time building tools: detection rules with real tests, a phishing response pipeline, cloud posture scanners, and guardrails for AI systems.
